UGC-style ads — a person talking directly to camera about a product — convert well because they read as authentic, not polished. AI actor tools let you produce this format without booking talent, at the cost of losing some of that authenticity if it's overused or poorly scripted.

## Step 1: Write the script like a real person would talk

The most common failure mode is writing ad copy instead of speech. Short sentences, a hook in the first line, one clear claim — not three. Read it out loud before generating; if it sounds like copywriting, rewrite it.

## Step 2: Pick an AI actor that matches your audience

Tools like Arcads offer a library of AI actors to choose from. Match the actor's apparent age, setting, and energy to who you're actually targeting — a mismatch is more noticeable in this format than in traditional video ads.

## Step 3: Generate and review for authenticity red flags

Watch the first render at full attention, not on fast-forward. Common tells: unnatural blinking, mismatched lip sync on fast dialogue, or overly clean audio with no room tone. Regenerate rather than publish something that reads as obviously synthetic — it undermines the exact thing this format is trying to achieve.

## Step 4: Localize if you're running the ad in multiple markets

A real advantage of this workflow over hiring actors: the same script can generate ad variations in multiple languages without sourcing native speakers in each market. Adjust idioms and cultural references per language rather than translating word-for-word.

## Step 5: Add captions and post

Run the finished video through an auto-captioning tool (Captions.ai handles this natively) before publishing — most viewers watch with sound off initially.

## FAQ

**Do I need to disclose that an ad uses an AI actor?**
Ad-platform and regional disclosure rules vary and change — check current requirements for your specific platform and market before publishing, rather than assuming.

**Will this replace real UGC entirely?**
For high-volume ad testing, it's a genuine cost advantage. Many marketers still mix AI-generated ads with real creator content rather than replacing one with the other.
