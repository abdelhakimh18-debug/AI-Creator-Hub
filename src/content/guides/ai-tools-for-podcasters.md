A podcast workflow typically breaks into three separate jobs — recording, editing, and voice/audio production — and different tools specialize in each rather than one tool doing everything well.

## Recording remote guests

Riverside.fm and Podcastle both record locally on each participant's device rather than streaming audio live, which avoids the choppy audio that comes from a bad internet connection during the call itself. Riverside's free tier caps at 2 hours per session; Podcastle's free tier is built around a lifetime allowance instead of a monthly reset — check which model fits your recording frequency.

## Editing the episode

Descript's text-based editing (cut filler words and tangents by editing the transcript, not a timeline) is well suited to podcast editing specifically, where most of the edit is removing dead air and repeated words rather than complex visual cuts. See [text-based video editing with Descript](/guides/text-based-video-editing-with-descript/) for the full workflow — it applies just as directly to audio-only podcasts.

## Cleaning up and generating voice content

ElevenLabs is useful beyond narration for podcasters specifically for two things: cleaning up a host's intro/outro with a consistent AI voice when re-recording isn't practical, and generating placeholder narration during editing before a final human pass.

## Repurposing episodes into clips

Once an episode is edited, running it through a clipping tool (see [best AI tools for TikTok and Reels creators](/guides/best-ai-tools-for-tiktok-and-reels-creators/)) turns the strongest 30–60 seconds into short-form promotional clips — the single highest-leverage repurposing step for podcast audience growth.
