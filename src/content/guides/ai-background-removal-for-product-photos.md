Clean product photography used to mean a lightbox and manual masking in Photoshop. AI background removal tools now do the masking step in seconds — the remaining work is making the result look intentional rather than obviously cut out.

## Step 1: Shoot with a plain, evenly lit background

AI background removal is more accurate on a plain background to begin with — a white wall, a solid-color backdrop, even a plain floor. Busy or low-contrast backgrounds increase the chance of ragged edges around fine details (hair, fabric texture, thin objects).

## Step 2: Remove the background and check the edges at full zoom

Zoom in on edges — especially anywhere the subject has fine detail — before moving on. This is the step people skip and regret; a clean-looking thumbnail can still have a visible halo or clipped detail at full size.

## Step 3: Replace with a background suited to the platform

A product listing usually wants pure white or a neutral studio background; a social post often benefits from a lifestyle or color background instead of plain white, which can look sterile in a feed. Match the background choice to where the image will actually be posted.

## Step 4: Batch-process consistent shots

If you're processing a full product catalog, batch tools save real time over doing each image individually — worth checking whether your plan includes batch/bulk processing before doing dozens by hand.

## Step 5: Export at the resolution your platform actually needs

Don't default to the largest export size available. Check the target platform's recommended image dimensions first — an oversized file just slows page load without adding visible quality.
