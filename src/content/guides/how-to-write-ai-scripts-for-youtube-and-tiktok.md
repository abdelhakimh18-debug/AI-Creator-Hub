AI writing tools are good at producing a first draft fast and bad at sounding like you if you let the first draft stand unedited. The workflow below treats the AI output as raw material, not a finished script.

## Step 1: Give it your actual speaking style, not just a topic

A prompt like "write a YouTube script about X" produces generic, forgettable copy. Feeding the tool a transcript of one of your own past videos — or a paragraph describing how you actually talk — gets noticeably closer to something usable.

## Step 2: Generate the hook separately from the body

The first 5–10 seconds of a video carry disproportionate weight for retention. Generate several hook options specifically, rather than accepting whichever opening line comes with the full script. The [YouTube Hook Prompt](/prompts/youtube-hook/) is built exactly for this step.

## Step 3: Cut anything that sounds like marketing copy

AI-generated scripts tend to default to a slightly promotional register — "game-changing," "unlock," "elevate." Read your draft out loud and cut every phrase you wouldn't actually say to a friend.

## Step 4: Rewrite the ending yourself

Calls-to-action are where AI-generated scripts sound most obviously generated. Write your own closing line — it's usually short enough that the time cost of doing it manually is negligible.

## Step 5: Read the final script aloud before filming

This catches awkward phrasing that reads fine on a page but is hard to say naturally — the single most useful edit pass in the whole process.
